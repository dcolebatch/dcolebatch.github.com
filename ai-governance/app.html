Opening setup
“I don't want this to become the usual AI panel where we catalogue risks, agree governance matters, and go home.”

AI creates real risks. But moving too slowly creates risks too.

Tonight I'd like us to separate what has happened from what we predict, compare AI with the processes it actually replaces, and make the trade-offs explicit.

Opening frame · Three layers of AI governance

“When we talk about AI governance, I find it useful to split it into three layers: what we control, inside our own companies; what we co-shape, across our customers, partners, suppliers and industries; and what we comply with, the legal and regulatory frameworks established by governments. They are different conversations and different levers. So, as we discuss the governance, risk and power of AI, let's be clear about which layer we're in and who has decision rights.”

CONTROLCorporate: internal policies, accountability, risk and controls.
CO-SHAPEMarket: contracts, customer expectations, industry standards and partners.
COMPLYPublic: legislation, regulation and government oversight.

Current labour-market read

September US payrolls: +29k and unemployment 4.2%. Genuine softness.

But: low separations and 1.0% layoff rate → the present pattern is more low-hire than mass-fire.

Cayman sovereignty

Cayman's DPA does not impose blanket on-island localization. International transfers require adequate protection / safeguards and remain the controller's responsibility.

AWS external key stores

External Key Store can keep the cryptographic root outside AWS. Disconnect the external key manager and KMS cannot perform decrypt operations with that key.

“For most workloads, the additional operational burden and greater risks to availability and performance will exceed the perceived security benefits of external key stores.”

AWS KMS External Key Store guidance. Governance lesson: maximum control ≠ optimum risk.

Rehearsal

Phrases that should roll off the tongue

“Exposure isn't displacement. Productivity isn't unemployment.”
Context: Census 2026 — 66% augmentation-only; 2% reported AI-related employment decreases.
“Right now the US labour market looks more low-hire than mass-fire.”
Context: Sep payrolls +29k, but Aug JOLTS layoffs/discharges only 1.0%.
“The thing I'd worry about first isn't mass unemployment. It's the bottom rung of the career ladder.”
Context: Stanford AI Index — ~16% relative employment decline among 22–25 year olds in most AI-exposed occupations.
“AI doesn't always replace the junior person. Sometimes it makes the junior person dramatically better.”
Context: NBER — +34% productivity for novice/lower-skilled customer-support workers.
“Fewer employees per company does not automatically mean fewer companies.”
Context: analytical thesis; directional support from 63% solo-founded Stripe Atlas C-corps in Q2 2026.
“If AI makes entrepreneurship cheaper, business-formation policy becomes AI policy.”
Context: Portugal +17% new firms after one-stop registration; Mexico +5% registered firms / +2.2% wage employment.
“Don't measure how quickly I get a certificate. Measure how quickly I can send an invoice and receive my first dollar.”
Context: policy metric — certificate + tax ID + bank/payment rail + invoice + first receipt.
“Capability isn't intent.”
Context: agent incidents can involve unauthorized behaviour without proving autonomous goals or sentience.
“The scary part isn't that the machine became alive. It's that we gave software enough authority to surprise us.”
Context: Australia Medicare incident — serious control/containment failure, not evidence of sentience.
“The strongest available control isn't automatically the right control.”
Context: AWS itself warns XKS availability/performance risks outweigh perceived security benefits for most workloads.
“You can make a system so sovereign that nobody can get to the data. The problem is that ‘nobody’ can eventually include you.”
Context: external-key control increases customer sovereignty and customer availability/recovery responsibility.
“Sovereignty isn't the same thing as geography. The questions that matter are access, control and accountability.”
Context: Cayman DPA permits international transfers with adequate protections; locality is only one sovereignty dimension.
“AI makes that even clearer: the asset can stay put while the capability crosses the border.”
Context: AI/trade framing — data can remain governed in place while inference, expertise or economic output crosses jurisdictions.
“The old trade model moved goods. AI lets us trade capability.”
Context: cross-jurisdiction framing for knowledge-intensive services and software exports.
“You don't necessarily have to export the data to export the value.”
Context: architecture/policy thesis — useful where controlled inference or services operate against locally governed data.
“We should be careful not to confuse local infrastructure with local capability.”
Context: physical hosting does not guarantee local skills, control or value capture.
“Sovereignty is an architecture decision, not a postcode.”
Context: jurisdiction, access, cryptographic control, audit and resilience are distinct.
“AI weakens the old assumption that economic value has to travel with the underlying asset. Increasingly, the asset can remain sovereign while the capability becomes an export.”
Context: Canada/Cayman cross-border AI and trade framing; thesis rather than measured statistic.

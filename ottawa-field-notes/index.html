Understand existing systems. Secure the foundations. Deliver useful capability in the field.

Category map

Six threads to pull

Each theme names a problem, its public relevance and a useful question. These are not descriptions of named commercial work.

01 · Legacy modernization

Frame: Updating critical systems begins with application ownership, dependencies and a realistic path away from fragile technology.

Consequence Reliability, change speed, cost and security all depend on understanding the existing estate.

“What should be measured: fewer failures, faster changes, reduced cost, or better service?”

Keep projected savings separate from realized results.

02 · Network visibility & cyber

Frame: Accurate IP address, subnet, asset and dependency inventories are basic enablers for security and modernization.

Consequence Unknown infrastructure creates avoidable incident-response and recovery blind spots.

“When something changes or fails, how quickly can teams establish what is affected?”
03 · Resilient compute & tactical communications

Frame: Some mission systems must operate despite limited, intermittent or unavailable connectivity.

Consequence Local processing, resilient identity and graceful synchronization can be as important as the backhaul.

“Which functions must continue when reach-back is unavailable, and for how long?”

Distinguish architectures, demonstrations and operationally fielded systems.

04 · Uncrewed air & maritime systems

Frame: Sensors, safe mission planning, assured communication, edge inference and autonomy need to work as a coordinated system.

Consequence Civilian and defence platforms can share technical requirements without sharing operational constraints.

“Where is the limiting factor: sensing, data processing, coordination, connectivity, or integration?”

A procurement qualification is eligibility to compete, not a contract. Official DDI background ↗

05 · Procurement & commercialization

Frame: Research funding, qualified supplier lists, pilots, purchase orders and field adoption are separate stages.

Consequence Strong technology does not automatically translate into timely operational procurement.

“Where does the path stall between an identified requirement and a funded deployment?”

Seek efficiency without assuming competition, security reviews or oversight can be skipped.

06 · Sovereignty, privacy & responsible AI

Frame: Data residency, legal jurisdiction, access, encryption-key ownership and service resilience are related but not interchangeable.

Consequence The correct hosting model depends on classification, obligations and operational requirements.

“Who can access the data, who controls keys, and what continues to work if the provider is unavailable?”

Six distinctions
Qualified ≠ contractedSupplier eligibility is not an awarded project.
Pilot ≠ deploymentFeasibility is not field readiness.
Forecast ≠ outcomeSeparate targets and measured results.
Residency ≠ sovereigntyLaw, access, keys and resilience matter.
Cloud ≠ always onlineMission continuity may need local systems.
Speed ≠ shortcutsSecurity and fair competition still count.

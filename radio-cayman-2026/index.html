Q2 · What is it?

Make the idea tangible.

“Let's start with Capture the Flag. What is the competition all about and how does it work?”

“Think of an escape room, but instead of physical locks, you're solving cybersecurity puzzles.”

We've built a fictional office with three floors and virtual locked doors. Participants explore specially created websites, discover hidden digital clues called flags, and earn points as they unlock challenges. There's a live scoreboard so everyone can follow along.

If asked what a flag is

It's a distinctive string of letters and numbers hidden in a simulated system. Finding it proves you solved that puzzle. You submit its cryptographic hash to earn points.

Q3 · Can beginners join?

Remove the intimidation factor.

“What types of challenges can participants expect and do they need previous experience in cybersecurity or coding to take part?”

“You don't need to know how to hack. You need curiosity, a laptop and a willingness to experiment.”

We start with a Training Room: look at a webpage's source or change a number in a URL to uncover a clue. From there, challenges get harder—login mistakes, hidden information, access controls and cryptography. There are easier and harder paths, so beginners and experienced people can both enjoy it.

Ana confirmed participants include first-timers, more experienced people, different ages and returning CyberSandbox members.

If asked about AI or teams

Teams can collaborate, and AI can be an investigative aid. The important part is not copying an answer but understanding why it works and verifying it.

Q4 · Skills gained

Translate puzzles into careers.

“What skills can participants develop through taking part in Capture the Flag?”

“We're teaching people to think like an attacker so they can become better defenders.”

It's about investigation, critical thinking and testing assumptions. You learn to spot how software can behave unexpectedly, work with teammates and explain what you've discovered. Those skills carry into cybersecurity, software engineering, IT operations and many other careers.

Q8 · Early-stage investing

Speak as a founder, not a stock picker.

“From your experience with investors, what are some of the key considerations when evaluating early-stage investment opportunities?”

“An exciting idea isn't necessarily a good investment. I look for evidence of a real customer problem, a team that can execute and a credible business model.”

My filter is demand, execution and economics. Who has the problem? Will customers pay? Can this team deliver? How will capital be used, and what's the path to a sustainable business? Valuation, dilution and downside risk matter just as much as the technology.

If pressed on AI in investing

AI makes research and comparison faster, but it doesn't replace due diligence. Test its claims against customer evidence, financial information and the team's actual execution record.
